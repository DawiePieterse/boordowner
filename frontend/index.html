Actual Harvest by Season

This score is built from a one-off correlation study of this farm's own harvest records against its weather history, reaching back to 1987: the four factors below were the ones that lined up best with a bigger or smaller harvest and that measure genuinely different things, rather than four ways of restating the same one. It is not a generic agronomy model, and it isn't recalculated live - the driver list itself only changes if this study is redone.

Each factor contributes up to 25 risk points, scaled between the best and worst value seen across the reference seasons (2012 onward, once the replanted orchard was bearing) - so 25/25 means "as bad as the worst season on file for that factor", not an absolute threshold. Points are summed to a 0-100 score once every factor's calendar window has closed for that season; a window still open just isn't counted yet (never assumed to be zero risk), which is why the current season shows a partial "score so far" until its last window closes.

Honest limits:

  • Weather explains roughly a quarter to a half of this farm's swing from season to season. That is real signal, but most of what makes a season good or bad is something this score cannot see - treat it as one input among several, not a verdict.
  • Many factor-and-window combinations were tested to arrive at these four, and in any such search some of what looks like signal is chance. These four were kept because they held up across separate stretches of the farm's history, not because they scored well once.
  • Contrary to what is often expected of lychees, this farm's own record shows no alternate-bearing pattern: across 35 back-to-back season pairs, last season's crop tells you essentially nothing about this one. An earlier version of this note claimed the opposite - the longer record does not support it.
