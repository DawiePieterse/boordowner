    Weather model cross-check

    Your estimate is block by block, from what you see in the orchard - fruit set, the strong and weak blocks. It knows nothing about the weather still to come.

    The weather model is the Risk tab's Harvest Forecast: a whole-farm figure from four weather factors, through a straight line fitted on past seasons (). It cannot see tree numbers, blocks coming into bearing, fruit set or pests. Weather explains roughly a quarter to a half of this farm's swing from season to season.

    Its figures are rounded to 500 kg and never go past the best or worst harvest on record since its fitted range began. Unfavorable combines each factor's own worst year; the Unfavorable-Favorable span is the model's worst- and best-weather cases, not an error band.

    Until a factor's window closes, the days still ahead use a real weather forecast (trusted less over about a week) or the historical range - "settled" counts the factors whose whole window is now measured weather - until then the three cases still differ on them.

    A gap between the two is a reason to look again in the orchard, not proof that either one is wrong. Both are net picked kg, not packed cartons. Each saved version keeps the model's figures as they were when it was saved.

    Similar past seasons

    Seasons are matched on the four weather factors the Risk tab scores, over the same calendar days in every season. A factor whose window is still open this season is compared only up to the last day of weather on file, and counts for less the fewer days are in - so the list will shift through October and November.

    Distance is in each factor's usual spread between seasons: under 0.5 is close, under 1 fair, beyond that loose. Weather explains roughly a quarter to a half of this farm's swing from season to season, so seasons with similar weather still came in very differently - read the range, not one figure. The Risk tab's Harvest Forecast (above) is the weather-driven kg figure.

    Old-orchard seasons (whole-farm totals only) are compared as a percentage of their own era's average and never used for block figures; seasons while the replanted orchard was still coming into bearing are left out. Last season's crop is deliberately not a factor: this farm's record shows no alternate bearing.

    Picking start is when a season reached 2% of its own total, and length runs from then to the last pick - descriptors only, for seasons with daily records. The weather is Open-Meteo's modelled grid for the farm's location, not the farm's own station.

    Actual Harvest by Season

    This score is built from a one-off correlation study of this farm's own harvest records against its weather history, reaching back to 1987: the four factors below were the ones that lined up best with a bigger or smaller harvest and that measure genuinely different things, rather than four ways of restating the same one. It is not a generic agronomy model, and it isn't recalculated live - the driver list itself only changes if this study is redone.

    Each factor contributes up to 25 risk points, scaled between the best and worst value seen across the reference seasons (2012 onward, once the replanted orchard was bearing) - so 25/25 means "as bad as the worst season on file for that factor", not an absolute threshold. Points are summed to a 0-100 score once every factor's calendar window has closed for that season; a window still open just isn't counted yet (never assumed to be zero risk), which is why the current season shows a partial "score so far" until its last window closes.

    Honest limits:

    • Weather explains roughly a quarter to a half of this farm's swing from season to season. That is real signal, but most of what makes a season good or bad is something this score cannot see - treat it as one input among several, not a verdict.
    • Many factor-and-window combinations were tested to arrive at these four, and in any such search some of what looks like signal is chance. These four were kept because they held up across separate stretches of the farm's history, not because they scored well once.
    • Contrary to what is often expected of lychees, this farm's own record shows no alternate-bearing pattern: across 35 back-to-back season pairs, last season's crop tells you essentially nothing about this one. An earlier version of this note claimed the opposite - the longer record does not support it.
